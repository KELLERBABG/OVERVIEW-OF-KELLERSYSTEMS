A1. [REFUTED] §1 header: "Simulation über 8.760 reale Jahresstunden (NRW) mit
    stündlichen Wetterdaten (Open-Meteo) und synchronen Spotmarktpreisen
    (EPEX Day-Ahead)."

    The weather is real. The prices are NOT. The market price series in the
    cache that drives every euro in the table is the program's own synthetic
    fallback generator.

    Evidence — I re-implemented the two generators from simulation.rs and
    compared them value-by-value with data_cache/fleet_2025.json:

        price matches synth_price()        : 8760 / 8760   (<1e-6)
        freq  matches grid_frequency()     : 8760 / 8760   (<1e-9)
        amb   matches synth_temp()         :    0 / 8760

    simulation.rs:51 is the smoking gun:
        let price = prices.get(&(h as u32)).copied()
                        .unwrap_or_else(|| synth_price(h, amb));
    i.e. whatever the API did or did not return, the cache written back contains
    synth_price() rows (simulation.rs:158).

    Cache statistics vs the real German 2025 day-ahead year:
        cache      : mean  57.8 EUR/MWh, min  -6.6, max 268.1, 126 negative hours
        real 2025  : mean  89.32 EUR/MWh, 573 negative hours, 40 h > 300 EUR/MWh
        (the "real 2025" figures are the ones YOUR OWN earlier audit quotes,
         SANITY_CHECK.md @ HEAD, section 3.4 "Rot: Preisdatensatz nicht das
         behauptete reale EPEX-Jahr")

    Corroborating: crates/gateway/data_cache/2025_dataset.json has
    "price_eur_per_mwh": null for all 8,760 hours (the gateway seed carries no
    prices at all), and the frequency series is synthetic by construction
    (simulation.rs:201).

    Consequence: §1's euro figures are a scenario result from a synthetic price
    curve, not an "EPEX Day-Ahead" result. The earlier, longer version of
    SANITY_CHECK.md already said this explicitly (section 3.4). The V2.1 rewrite
    has reintroduced a claim that your own audit had refuted. This is the single

A4. [REFUTED] §2 reason 1: "Offene Kühlregale und Glastüren haben eine sehr
    kleine thermische Zeitkonstante (τ ≈ 1–2 Stunden). Die Temperatur steigt
    beim Abschalten sofort an."

    The model's own time constant is τ = r_th · c_th (physics.rs:31-34,
    r_th from u·A + infiltration, c_th = thermal_mass_kj_k · 1000):

        aldi       τ = 1,899 h        edeka   τ = 1,288 h
        marktkauf  τ =   922 h        freezer_small τ = 1,944 h
        brewery_mid τ = 6,765 h       dc_large τ = 308 h

    These are not "1–2 hours", they are three orders of magnitude larger. In
    simulate_hour the per-hour temperature step is governed by
    (1 − exp(−1 h/τ)), so a shed hour moves the zone by roughly 0.03 K
    (physics.rs:151 correction term, ±0.08 VFD, over τ ≈ 1,900 h). "Die
    Temperatur steigt beim Abschalten sofort an" is the opposite of what this
    code does. The stated mechanism is not the mechanism.

A5. [REFUTED as the visible mechanism] §2 reason 2: the Carnot "Rückholverlust"
    after a shed "vernichtet den finanziellen Arbitragegewinn".

    There is no recovery penalty in the financial model and, per A4, no
    meaningful rebound temperature to recover from. The retail losses are an
    ENERGY effect: for aldi the run reports actual 208.3 MWh against a baseline
    of 199.9 MWh (+4.2 %), and actual cost 11,448 EUR against baseline
    11,411 EUR. The driver is the VFD "thermal battery" correction term
    (physics.rs:151, temp_error × 0.05, ±8 %) plus the ASCT governor lock
    (control.rs:39-58), not differential COP during recovery. Attribute the
    negative result to the controller's own energy overhead, not to Carnot.

B4. [CONFIRMED/CAVEAT] §1 note "Bei Anlagen mit thermischen Speichern [TES]
    ... kommen zusätzliche FCR-Regelleistungserlöse hinzu."
    Code: compute_fcr_revenue returns 0 unless t.has_tes, and exactly the
    brewery_* and dc_* types have has_tes = true (store_types.rs) — so the note
    is code-accurate. Its MAGNITUDE is the problem: in my run the twelve-type
    fleet earned 718,693 EUR FCR+Droop against 284,163 EUR of energy savings,
    i.e. the "additional" income is 2.5x the business case. It is computed from
    the synthetic frequency series (8760/8760 match, A1) as
    capacity_MW × 15 EUR/MW × response_fraction(0.3/0.8) × 0.5 event factor,
    per hour with |Δf| > 0.01 Hz (control.rs compute_fcr_revenue). Real FCR is a
    capacity product with tendered prices and metered delivery; this model pays
    an hourly event fee on nameplate cooling power and never checks whether the
    machine actually responded. Also, for TES types the tool's own headline box
    uses (savings + FCR)/2 — it prints 82K EUR/yr for brewery_large while §1's
    50/50 column says 44,310 EUR for the same machine. The document must pick
    one definition.

B8. [CONFIRMED/CAVEAT] §3.1 COV filtering + heartbeat exist in code.
    modbus_client.rs:403-437 compares last_written_speed / _coil / _alarm before
    writing; modbus_client.rs:441-445 writes heartbeat_counter.wrapping_add(1)
    each successful poll. Both are real and correct.
    Caveats that the document should state:
    - ModbusRegisters::default (config.rs:349) leaves compressor_speed_addr and
      heartbeat_addr as None, and the shipped config.toml declares
      [modbus] enabled = false with only internal_temp_addr, compressor_cmd_addr,
      ambient_temp_addr, alarm_addr. So in the delivered configuration the speed
      COV filter and the heartbeat are INACTIVE; only coil + alarm COV would run
      once an operator wires Modbus up.
    - "Per-connection write cache": on every reconnect the cache is empty and all
      three values are rewritten once.
    - config.toml sets vfd_enabled = true, so the commanded speed is a
      proportional-integral function of temperature and changes on most polls;
      the register COV therefore saves far fewer writes than the EEPROM argument
      implies. The COV claim is strongest for the coil (that part is exact).
    - The hardware watchdog ([watchdog] enabled = false in config.toml) is also
      off in the shipped configuration.

B9. [CONFIRMED/CAVEAT] §2 reason 3 (cargo core temperature).
    The cargo lag model exists (physics.rs, cargo_r_th = r_th × 0.1,

    in the fleet controller — only air temperature is clamped to [t_min, t_max]

    gateway it does feed the safety logic (cargo_lag_enabled, cargo_t_max).

C1. §4 "Der gesamte Software-Workspace ist versioniert".
    PARTIAL. All 11 files that implement the "correction" (the harmonised
    baseline, the 12 new tier types, the peak-window gate, the gateway slew /
    derate / heartbeat / COV additions) and SANITY_CHECK.md itself are
    UNCOMMITTED working-tree changes. The cache the tool actually consumes when
    run from the repo root is /data_cache/, which .gitignore line 19 excludes;
    only crates/fleet/data_cache/fleet_2025.json is tracked (the two files differ
    in bytes though they agree on the values I checked). benchmark CSVs are also
    ignored (**/fleet_results_v2_*.csv). So the exact state that produced §1 is
    in no commit.

C2. The "Strombedarf (Jahr)" column is compressor electricity only.
    financial.rs:69 costs only actual_energy_mwh; the defrost heater energy is
    accumulated but never priced (financial.rs:75) and fan energy is never costed
    (physics.rs:159). The run itself prints the excluded defrost energy: aldi
    43.8 MWh (+21 % on top of the 208 MWh shown), freezer_small 21.9 MWh (+14 %),
    freezer_large 292 MWh (+26 %). Additionally a 0.5 h defrost is charged a full
    hour of heater energy (physics.rs:122 executes per whole-hour step), which is
    why aldi reports 43.8 MWh/yr instead of ~21.9 MWh/yr. If the column is meant
    to be "Jahresstrombedarf", it is understated by 14–26 %.

C3. Cost basis is spot-only.
    Both baseline and actual are priced at the hourly spot price
    (financial.rs:66-69) with no grid fees, levies, taxes or supplier margin.
    That is internally consistent, but it means "5–25 % of annual refrigeration
    electricity COST" cannot be read off this table — all-in industrial cost is
    typically 2–4x the spot component, and the saving fraction of an all-in
    invoice would be correspondingly smaller.

C4. §1's monthly column.
    11 of 12 rows are the savings column / 24 to within rounding, but
    brewery_small is stated as "~155 €/Mo." while its own row gives 1,887/12 =
    157.25 (and the table rounds 1,887 from 1,887.17). Minor, but an auditor will
    find it.

C5. §0.2c and §2.1 contradict each other and the code.
    §2.1 needs a tiny τ so that the temperature "rises immediately"; §0.2c needs
    a large rebound so that the "Carnot-Kater" eats the profit. The code has
    τ ≈ 1,000–6,800 h, which rules out both stories (A4/A5). One physical
    narrative has to go.
