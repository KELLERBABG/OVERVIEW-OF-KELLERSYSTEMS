Physical Dynamics & Actuator RealitiesOmission of Suspension & Dynamic Load Transfer (Pitch/Roll): The current 2D single-track (bicycle) model handles longitudinal load transfer, but omits roll/pitch dynamics. In high-speed evasive swerves, lateral load transfer significantly reduces inner-wheel normal forces before slipping occurs, leading to potential premature rollover or sudden loss of grip not predicted by planar dynamics.Actuator Latency & Rate Delays: The invariant check validates steering rate ($\Delta\delta / \Delta t$) and brake pressure bounds, but does not explicitly account for lower-level actuator lag (e.g., hydraulic build-up time or CAN-bus latency). If an "ALLOW" decision is issued, the physical vehicle state will lag behind the theoretical trajectory, introducing phase margin degradation.2. Abstract Interpretation & Interval Domain ExplosionWrapping Effect / Dependency Problem: Pure interval domains ($[lo, hi]$) suffer heavily from the dependency problem ($x - x \neq 0$). When evaluating chained physical equations (such as numerical integration across multiple time steps in dynamics-simulator), interval widths explode exponentially over longer horizons, leading to excessive false positives (spurious "BLOCK" decisions).Mitigation: Consider upgrading from simple Intervals to Octagons or Polyhedra, or using Affine Arithmetic to track variable dependencies across dynamic steps.3. SMT & Collision Model SimplificationsConstant-Velocity Horizon Assumption: Assumptions of constant velocity over the time horizon in swept-circle calculations can yield incorrect results during aggressive maneuvers. A vehicle rapidly braking will over-estimate its swept path, while an accelerating vehicle will under-estimate its collision threat horizon.Rigid Circular Swept Bounds: Approximating long or wide commercial vehicles with simple circles creates severe geometry inaccuracies at sharp angles or during jackknifing/articulated scenarios.4. Edge Cases & Fail-Safe StrategyLack of Safe Fallback Trajectory Generation: When GateVerdict::Block is returned, the system relies on an external fallback or simply logs a warning. In safety-critical ASIL-D design, a safety gate should ideally output or trigger a verified Minimum Risk Maneuver (MRM) (e.g., maximum controlled deceleration within friction limits) rather than leaving the safe-state transition undefined.Taint Sanitize / Threshold Fallbacks: If sensor taint drops due to transient GPS/Camera spoofing or dropout, dropping directly into a "BLOCK" state without a degraded operation mode (e.g., blind limp-mode using IMU/Wheel speed dead-reckoning) could cause unnecessary hard stops in high-speed highway driving.5. Tooling & Ecosystem IntegrationManual SMT-LIB2 Serializer Overhead: Generating raw SMT-LIB2 strings for external solvers (like Z3) in real-time control pathways creates string allocation/parsing overhead. Direct FFI bindings (or relying strictly on the native analytical TCC solver for runtime while reserving SMT-LIB2 for offline verification) is essential for determinism.

Truck-Specific Physics & DynamicsHeavy commercial trucks behave radically differently from standard passenger vehicles:Air Brake Latency: Unlike hydraulic car brakes, pneumatic truck brakes have a noticeable mechanical lag ($\Delta t \approx 200\text{--}500\text{ ms}$) before full braking force is realized. If your state machine or SMT collision checker assumes instant brake pressure application, the real vehicle will overshoot its predicted stopping distance.Articulated Dynamics (Jackknifing & Off-Tracking): For semi-trucks, a single-track bicycle model is insufficient. You need trailer joint angles ($\theta_{\text{hitch}}$) and off-tracking constraints in your VehicleState and PathConstraints to prove that the trailer sweeping across lanes won't cause a side collision during turns.  High Center of Gravity & Rollover Bounds: Rollover is often a higher risk for loaded trucks than sliding/skidding. Your LateralAccelerationInvariant needs to dynamically scale with estimated cargo weight/CoG height.  B. "No Self-Caused Collisions" Formal Model (RSS Framework)To prove you cannot crash unless someone drives into you, your SMT solver and invariant definitions need a formal model of fault-free safety (similar to Mobileye's Responsibility-Sensitive Safety model):Safe Longitudinal Following Distance: Proofs must account for the worst-case reasonable deceleration of the lead vehicle.Non-Giver-of-Way Assertions: Your SMT solver must mathematically differentiate between ego vehicle cutting someone off (unsafe command, MUST block) vs. another vehicle making an illegal lane change into ego's safe zone (external fault).Right-of-Way Formalism: Right now, the SMT queries treat all obstacles as moving spheres/boxes. You'll want to incorporate map/lane topology and right-of-way rules into path-constraints to formally define who is at fault in intersection/merge scenarios.  C.
