01 — the mechanismDeterministic scope nullifiers

In a pseudonymous network one actor can spin up ten thousand addresses, and no amount of rate limiting distinguishes them from ten thousand people. SybilGuard makes the distinction inside the circuit, where it cannot be inspected or forged.

A person's secret is fixed, but the identifier they present is derived per scope. In a governance vote the nullifier is a function of the scope and nothing else, so a second ballot from the same person produces the identical nullifier and is rejected as a double action.

Across two different scopes the nullifiers share no value, so the two actions cannot be joined up. Voting in one protocol does not reveal that you voted in another.

The prover shows membership in the verified personhood Merkle tree. The verifier learns the nullifier and the scope identifier — a claim of uniqueness, and nothing about who made it.

// derived inside the circuit, not by the application
Nullifier  = Poseidon(IdentitySecret, ScopeIdentifier)
Commitment = Poseidon(IdentitySecret, Salt)

// what the verifier receives

    public
 = [ Nullifier, ScopeIdentifier ]   // second action: same nullifier, rejected

02 — the circuitsThree components on BN254

Verified in sub-second execution on mobile devices, and directly on EVM chains through Groth16 pairing precompiles.

CircuitWhat it enforces
merkle_proof.circomA twenty-level binary Poseidon Merkle tree inclusion check, approximately 4,800 R1CS constraints.
nullifier_derivation.circomScope-bound derivation, which is what prevents correlation across applications.
enclave_attestation.circomVerifies an Apple Secure Enclave or Android StrongBox ECDSA P-256 signature chain.
