01 — the graphEvery corridor is an edge with a cost

Road, rail and maritime legs are modelled as a weighted directed acyclic graph. An edge is not just distance: it carries transit time, fuel burn, carbon, and border clearance delay, so the cheapest path for a load is rarely the shortest one on a map.

The empty return is the expensive part. A trailer that delivers to Munich and drives back to Hamburg burns fuel carrying nothing, and that cost is priced into every load. Pairing a return leg against an outbound one is what removes 37.2% of that distance — the amber and blue routes above are the same vehicle doing both.

02 — the clearingRates without a broker, and without disclosure

A carrier will not quote honestly if the quote reveals what its costs are, and a shipper will not bid openly if the bid reveals its budget. ASCA clears rates in a multi-party computation: participants submit sealed values, the winning price is computed across shares, and nobody learns a competitor's number — only whether they won and at what price.
